## Nepal federal budget — high-value legal-rule candidate, HOLD

Nepal is the strongest non-monetary South Asian candidate found in this pass.

Authoritative evidence:

- Nepal Ministry of Finance records the FY 2083/84 budget statement on **15 Jestha 2083**: https://www.mof.gov.np/content/1741/budget8384/
- Nepal Ministry of Finance budget-speech index likewise records the Nepali-calendar publication date for the 2026/27 budget: https://www.mof.gov.np/category/budget-speech/
- Nepal National Planning Commission publishes the associated medium-term expenditure framework / annual development programme on the same Nepali-calendar date for FY 2083/84: https://npc.gov.np/category/621/

The constitutional/legal budget rule is therefore sufficiently authoritative to treat **15 Jestha** as a real recurring fiscal boundary in Nepal's native calendar.

### Why it is not canonical yet

WORLD SIGNALS does not currently possess an authoritative Nepal-government conversion surface that uniquely maps **15 Jestha 2084** to the required 2027 Gregorian date.

A third-party Bikram Sambat converter would create a hidden external-calendar dependency and would violate the rule against invented or weakly derived dates.

**Disposition:** `HIGH_VALUE_LEGAL_RULE_MONITOR / HOLD_GREGORIAN_CONVERSION_PROVENANCE`.

Do not create a Gregorian canonical occurrence until the conversion chain itself has authoritative provenance.

## Interaction with physical-climate-risk work

South Asia has obvious cyclone/monsoon exposure, but that is **not** repaired here by adding climatological season folklore.

The IMD/RSMC New Delhi source describes the North Indian Ocean cyclone climatology using **two month-bounded phases (April–June and October–December)**. That candidate is held under the separate physical-risk ontology audit until canonical timing can represent non-contiguous, month-precision seasonal windows without inventing first/last civil dates.
