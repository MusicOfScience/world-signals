## Evidence carried forward

The packet uses four primary official sources — the 29 September RBA monetary-policy decision, the October RBA Financial Stability Review global chapter, the RBA household/business resilience chapter, and the ABS August job-vacancies release — plus the ABC markets live blog as secondary discovery/context only.

The primary-source evidence supports the following bounded starting state:

- global energy disruption is adding to Australian inflation pressure;
- global sovereign-bond and risk-asset vulnerabilities remain a potential financing-condition spillover channel;
- the RBA cash-rate target is 4.60 per cent after the 29 September increase;
- housing prices have fallen in most capital cities and new housing loans have declined noticeably;
- mortgage cash-flow compression is visible for a minority of borrowers;
- household equity and liquidity buffers remain substantial and arrears remain low;
- job vacancies have eased, with private vacancies down and public vacancies up over the latest quarter;
- daily ASX, bond-yield and oil-curve observations from the ABC blog remain contextual market sensors, not canonical support.

## Candidate transmission structure

### R1 — energy → inflation → monetary constraint

Candidate class: `MECHANISTICALLY_SUPPORTED`.

The RBA explicitly identifies the global energy shock and fuel-price pass-through in its inflation assessment and September tightening decision. This is the strongest candidate relationship in the packet, but it remains unadmitted.

### R2 — restrictive financial conditions → housing / household cash flow

Candidate class: `HYPOTHESISED_TRANSMISSION`.

The evidence is consistent with the expected chain through borrowing capacity, mortgage service, housing-market weakness and household cash flow. The packet does not claim that every link has already met the repository's production Relationship standard.

### R3 — housing → labour → balance-sheet feedback

Candidate class: `HYPOTHESISED_TRANSMISSION`.

This is a prospective watch chain rather than an established feedback loop. Labour-market deterioration is the principal transition variable to monitor before moving from cash-flow compression to broad balance-sheet stress.

## Candidate Australian risk state

`AU_MONETARY_HOUSING_COMPRESSION`

- **Stage 1 — ACTIVE:** affordability / housing-market deterioration.
- **Stage 2 — EMERGING:** household cash-flow compression.
- **Stage 3 — NOT ESTABLISHED:** balance-sheet / forced-sale feedback.

The Stage 3 restraint is important. The RBA reports low arrears, less than 1 per cent of borrowers in negative equity, and a median mortgagor buffer above one year of scheduled payments. These are explicit disconfirmers against a premature crisis narrative.

## Competing draft scenarios

1. **Controlled adjustment** — energy/inflation pressure recedes, labour remains resilient and housing stabilises before Stage 3 develops.
2. **Stagflationary squeeze** — imported energy inflation continues to constrain monetary relief while housing and domestic demand weaken.
3. **Employment-triggered housing feedback** — labour deterioration weakens debt service, arrears/forced sales rise, and housing weakness feeds back into demand.

These are conditional pathways. They are not ranked and carry no future-outcome probabilities.
