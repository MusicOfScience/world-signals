## What happened

The umbrella June Climate Meetings closed on 18 June after the two subsidiary bodies met from 8–18 June. UNFCCC's own later synthesis describes the substantive results as mixed: progress in several areas, insufficient progress in others, and significant divides still requiring intersessional work.

The strongest post-session fact for WORLD SIGNALS is not a scalar success/failure judgment. It is the institutional structure of the outputs:

- each subsidiary body retained its own report;
- each generated formal post-session documentation;
- addenda contain draft decisions forwarded to higher UNFCCC decision bodies;
- forwarding creates an operational dependency for later consideration but does not equal adoption.

## What was expected

The official SBI/SBSTA session pages and agendas established a broad portfolio of negotiations, implementation work and mandated dialogues. They establish process scope and institutional tasks, not a defensible forecast that all or any specified proportion of issues would be resolved by 18 June.

AO therefore uses one qualitative `OFFICIAL_PRIOR_GUIDANCE` benchmark for session scope and mandated workstreams. It does not fabricate a forecast from agenda length or mandate wording.

## Noise and alternatives

Potential analytical noise:

- reducing the entire conference to `success` or `failure`;
- treating `mixed results` as `MIXED surprise`;
- using the count of agenda items or draft decisions as a scalar progress score;
- treating the Executive Secretary's statement as a consensus decision text;
- treating late closure/plenary suspension as substantive failure without evidence;
- treating August report publication as the event date.

Alternative explanations for uneven progress include substantive national-interest differences, consensus procedures, technical complexity, deliberate sequencing into intersessional work and higher-body decision stages, and agenda-specific maturity differences. AO does not infer which dominates without stronger evidence.
